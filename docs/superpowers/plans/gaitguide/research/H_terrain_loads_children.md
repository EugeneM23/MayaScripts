## 1. Slopes

### Walking, self-selected speed, with numbers

| Study | Condition | Speed | Step / stride | Timing | Joints |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lu 2023, n=28, self-paced treadmill | level / +8° / −8° | 1.35 / 1.07 / 1.34 m/s | step 0.71 / 0.61 / 0.69 m | stride time 1.06 / 1.16 / 1.03 s; stance 0.69 / 0.77 / 0.65 s; swing 0.37 / 0.39 / 0.38 s | — |
| Shimotori 2026, n=20 young, 7° ramp overground | level / up / down | 1.39 / 1.24 / 1.27 m/s | step 0.73 / 0.72 / 0.67 m | cadence 114 / 104 / 113 steps/min | hip stance max flexion 24 / 43 / 22°; knee loading-response peak 14 / 25 / 24°; knee swing peak 66 / 61 / 70°; ankle at contact −2 / +1 / 0° (− = plantarflexion) |

Sources: Lu 2023 https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10280335/fullTextXML ; Shimotori 2026 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13128050/

Derived from Lu's stride times (my arithmetic): cadence ≈ 113 (level), 103 (+8°), 117 (−8°) steps/min.

The two studies agree on the pattern:
- **Uphill** at ~8°: about −11 to −21 % speed and −9 % cadence. Step length is −1 % overground and −14 % on the self-paced treadmill.
- **Downhill** at ~8°: speed −0 to −9 %, cadence unchanged or slightly up, step length −3 to −8 %.

Another self-paced treadmill study (n=7, ±9°) found the order level 1.15 > decline 1.06 > incline 0.92 m/s, and no single steady speed across slopes **(snippet)**: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10280335/ (search result summary; the UCF thesis https://stars.library.ucf.edu/etd/6279).

At a **fixed** treadmill speed (5 km/h), slope from 0 to 15 % barely changes step length (78.6 → 79.2 cm) or cadence (106 → 105 steps/min). The shortening at self-selected speed comes from walking slower: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9863443/ (Mexi 2023, Table 2).

### Trunk, pelvis, joints (direction; few numbers)

- **Trunk lean uphill.**
  - +10° treadmill at 75 % of preferred speed: peak trunk flexion in loading response ≈ 12.6° against ≈ 4.9° on level (healthy controls, Gaffney 2021). That is roughly +8°. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8845490/
  - 10 % gradient (≈5.7°) at 4 km/h: trunk forward lean +4.4° in all gait phases (Hinde 2017). https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5346415/fullTextXML
- **Leroux 2002** (0 to ±10 % treadmill), uphill:
  - hip, knee and ankle more flexed at initial contact;
  - trunk and pelvis tilt forward progressively;
  - pelvic lateral drop decreases; stride length increased.
- **Leroux 2002, downhill**:
  - the trunk tilts backward;
  - stride shortens;
  - pelvic lateral drop increases.
- **Leroux 2002, standing** on the slope: the trunk stays vertical. Abstract only (no degrees): https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Trunk%20angle%20during%20flat%20and%20inclined%20walking%22%20OR%20TITLE:%22Postural%20adaptation%20to%20walking%20on%20inclined%20surfaces%22&resultType=core&format=json
- **Leu/Lu 2012**, 0/5/10/15°: anterior pelvic tilt and trunk flexion grow with each step of incline **(snippet; no degrees)**: https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/464503
- **McIntosh 2006**, ±5/8/10°, self-selected speed:
  - hip flexion at heel strike rises steadily from −10° to +10°;
  - knee flexion and dorsiflexion at heel strike rise uphill but NOT downhill.
  Abstract: same Europe PMC query family (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22gait%20dynamics%20on%20an%20inclined%20walkway%22&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Dewolf 2018**, −9 to +9°, 0.56 to 2.22 m/s:
  - uphill, the largest changes come at foot contact (more hip, knee and ankle flexion);
  - downhill, the main change is more knee flexion through stance;
  - the trunk tilts forward uphill and back downhill;
  - stride period hardly changes with slope at a given speed.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6842866
- **Lay 2006**, steep ramps up to ±39 % (≈21°), constant speed: uphill is hip-extensor-driven, downhill knee-extensor (braking) driven. Abstract only: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:15990102&resultType=core&format=json
- **Kuster 1995**, −19 % ramp: much higher knee moment and power downhill. Abstract only, link as above (Kuster query).

### Running on slopes (from a 2025 review; originals not opened)

- **7 % uphill** at 4.17 m/s (Padulo 2013): cadence +4.5 %, stride −4.3 %, flight time −13.7 %.
- **Ground forces at 3 m/s** (Gottschall & Kram 2005):
  - −9° downhill: impact peak +54 %, braking peak +73 %;
  - +9° uphill: propulsive peak +75 %.
- **Foot strike**: uphill shifts runners toward mid/forefoot strikes; downhill increases rearfoot strikes.
- **Knee**: more knee flexion at touchdown downhill.

All from https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592170/ (Lu 2025).

### Animator takeaways — slopes

1. **Uphill 8-10°**:
   - slow the walk 10-20 % and drop cadence ~10 %;
   - shorten the step 0-14 % (less on a gentle ramp);
   - pitch the trunk forward an extra 4-8° (5° gradient: +4°; 10° slope: +8°).
2. **Uphill contact**:
   - hip flexed ~18° more at peak (24 → 43° at 7°);
   - knee clearly bent, ~25° in loading against 14°;
   - foot near flat (ankle about neutral) rather than heel-first plantarflexed;
   - swing knee slightly less (66 → 61°); the leg lifts by the hip.
3. **Downhill 7-8°**:
   - keep cadence, shorten the step 3-8 %;
   - trunk upright or slightly back;
   - knee bends deeper after contact (14 → 24°) as the braking move;
   - more swing knee (66 → 70°);
   - pelvis drops more on the swing side.
4. **Running**: uphill — shorter, quicker strides, toes first. Downhill — heel first, knee bent at touchdown, a heavier impact.

## 2. Stairs

Riser / tread used in the studies:
- 18 / 28.5 cm (Protopapadaki 2007, ≈32°);
- 17 / 28 cm (Park 2019; Spanjaard 2008);
- 20 / 30 cm (Komnik 2018);
- 22 / 28 cm (McFadyen & Winter 1988);
- 24°, 30°, 42° flights (Riener 2002).

Sources:
- Park 2019: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6817922/fullTextXML
- Protopapadaki 2007 and Riener 2002, abstracts: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Stair%20ascent%20and%20descent%20at%20different%20inclinations%22&resultType=core&format=json
- Komnik 2018: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6179266/fullTextXML
- Spanjaard 2008: https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/211/9/1368/18222/Lower-limb-biomechanics-during-stair-descent

Other findings:
- **Foot landing.** Riener 2002: the **forefoot strikes first in both ascent and descent**, at every inclination. Spanjaard: in descent the toe lands first, then the heel comes down by ankle dorsiflexion against active plantarflexors. Higher steps put more plantarflexion at touchdown and a shorter stance.
- **Inclination.** Riener: the temporal cycle and ground forces do not change significantly with stair inclination; joint angles change little.
- **Trunk.** Max trunk flexion in ascent is about double that in descent and ~6× level walking. Ascending, the trunk is held roughly parallel to the flight in older subjects (Livingston 1995, n=6 **(snippet)**): https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/2996
- **Ascent cadence**: one pilot reports ≈ 76 steps/min ascent and 117 descent with no phone **(snippet; stair size unknown)**: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687019302182
- **Handrail and leading leg**: not found. The lab studies are step-over-step, mostly without a handrail.

### Animator takeaways — stairs

1. **Timing**: one step per tread at ~90 steps/min (≈ 0.65 s a step) against ~113 on flat. Ascent stance is longer than descent (0.80 vs 0.70 s).
2. **Ascent**:
   - swing the knee up to ~95° and the hip to ~50° to clear the riser;
   - place the forefoot or ball first on the tread;
   - lean the trunk forward clearly more than in a walk;
   - the push comes from knee extension.
3. **Descent**:
   - hip much less flexed (~30°);
   - knee still to ~90°;
   - land toe first with the ankle plantarflexed ~25°, then lower the heel (dorsiflexion to ~25°) as the knee bends to brake;
   - trunk near upright.
4. **Taller risers**: more plantarflexion at touchdown in descent, shorter contacts.

### Animator takeaways — loads

1. **Backpack**:
   - below ~15 % BW, adults change little;
   - at 20-30 % BW, pitch the trunk forward ≥ 10°, push the head forward, steps slightly quicker;
   - at 40 % BW, the pelvis and chest twist less, so steps are shorter and faster;
   - children lean earlier: about 13 → 18° trunk flexion from 5 to 15 % BW.
2. **Box or bucket in front**: lean the trunk back (lumbar extension) to put the load over the feet; steps ~2-3 % shorter and quicker; no arm swing.
3. **One hand** (suitcase, bucket, 10-20 % BW):
   - bend the trunk sideways AWAY from the load;
   - narrow the step width a little;
   - the free arm abducts out to the side as a counterweight;
   - the loaded arm hangs straight with no swing.

## 4. School-age children 6-12

Japan, n=424, 3D gait lab, values are medians; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9098504/ (Ito 2022, Tables 1-5):

| Age group | Height | Leg length | Speed | Cadence | Step length | Step / leg length | Knee swing peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6-8 y (median 7) | 124 cm | 0.59 m | 1.09 m/s | 130 steps/min | 0.50 m | 0.85 | 60.8° |
| 9-10 y | 135 cm | 0.66 m | 1.21 m/s | 127 | 0.57 m | 0.86 | 61.0° |
| 11-12 y | 148 cm | 0.75 m | 1.22 m/s | 122 | 0.60 m | 0.81 | 58.0° |

Same groups:
- hip stance max flexion 37 / 38 / 36°;
- ankle stance dorsiflexion 15 / 14 / 14°;
- swing plantarflexion ≈ −17 to −18°.

Speed normalised to leg length does not differ between the groups. The kinematic pattern is similar from 6 to 12; hip and knee excursions shrink slightly in the late grades.

Other sources:
- **Dusing & Thorpe 2007**, GAITRite, ages 1-10, n=438:
  - velocity 82 → 134 cm/s;
  - cadence falls and step length grows with age;
  - base of support about constant at 8.8 cm.
- **Lythgo 2009**, n=898, ages 5-13 + adults:
  - normalised measures do not change with age in children;
  - adults have +5.1 % double support, −2.3 % single support, +2.6 % stance;
  - conclusion: "gait may not be mature by age 13".
- **Lythgo 2011**: slow walk = −24 % of free speed, fast = +30 %.
- Lythgo and Dusing abstracts: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22normative%20sample%20of%20temporal%20and%20spatial%20gait%20parameters%20in%20children%22%20OR%20TITLE:%22Basic%20gait%20and%20symmetry%20measures%20for%20primary%20school-aged%20children%22&resultType=core&format=json
- **Froehle 2013** (Fels longitudinal, ages 8-30), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3580126:
  - the textbook view is gait maturity by 7-8 y (Sutherland 1988, Dusing 2007), and self-selected speed is indeed adult by ~8 y;
  - but leg growth runs to 13.2 y (girls) / 15.6 y (boys);
  - limb-length-dependent measures mature later: normalised cadence at ~14 y; normalised step length at 12.5 y (girls) / 16.4 y (boys); double / single support at 14.9 / 17.9 y;
  - immature walkers take more frequent and relatively longer steps.
- **Hillman 2009**: walk ratio stabilises from ~7-11 y; children change speed mainly through cadence. https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/19335/

### Animator takeaways — children

1. **A 7-year-old** (≈124 cm): ~130 steps/min, a 50 cm step, ~1.1 m/s, against an adult's ~110-115 and ~70 cm. **An 11-12-year-old**: ~122 steps/min, 60 cm.
2. **Relative to leg length** a child's step is the same or slightly longer (0.85 leg lengths) than an adult's. Scale the step with the legs and raise the cadence: the "busy" look comes from frequency.
3. **Shape**: from ~7 the joint curves are adult-shaped (knee swing ~60°, ankle as an adult's). Give slightly less double support than an adult. Do not animate a toddler pattern for a school child.
4. **Speed change**: a child speeds up by stepping faster more than by stepping longer.

## 5. Other surfaces and directions (brief)

- **Backward walking** (Cadenas-Sanchez 2015, cadence fixed by metronome, n=8), https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4723164/fullTextXML:
  - speed 0.88 → 0.58 m/s and stride 1.23 → 0.90 m;
  - stance 66 → 69 %;
  - at contact the knee is ~14° bent instead of ~2°, the ankle ~19° plantarflexed (toe lands first), the hip 7° instead of 17°.
  - Xiao: at self-selected cadence, cadence also drops and knee ROM is smaller **(snippet)**: https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/en/wpr-804477
- **Water to the chest** (same study): speed 0.62 m/s; stride 0.90 m; stance 61 %; knee ~12° bent and hip 23° at contact.
- **Ice**, slippery walkway with friction ≈ 0.06 (Cappellini 2010 abstract), https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Motor%20patterns%20during%20walking%20on%20a%20slippery%20walkway%22&resultType=core&format=json:
  - shorter steps and cycle, less horizontal force;
  - a "non-plantigrade" foot;
  - more arm movement, trunk rotation and lateral trunk lean.
  - Another study: a flattened foot with more hip and knee flexion **(snippet)**: https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-mechanics/article/modified-gait-patterns-during-stepping-on-slippery-floor/053EAD7F06B5E8BCDD7288C3A4161C95
- **Sand**:
  - Lejeune 1998: 1.6-2.5× mechanical work and 2.1-2.7× energy for walking (abstract, same query as ice).
  - Grant 2022, on soft foam: speed 1.36 → 1.23 m/s; longer stride, stance and duty factor; narrower stride width; more hip and knee flexion at heel strike and in swing. https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9709563/fullTextXML

Takeaways:
- **Backward**: toe-first contact, bent knee, ~30 % shorter strides.
- **Ice**: short flat-footed steps, knees soft, arms out.
- **Sand**: slower, the knee and hip lifted higher, a longer stance.
- **Water**: slow-motion timing, a bent knee at contact.
