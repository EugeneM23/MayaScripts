## Mathematical models of gait research

subagent_type: general-purpose

You are researching for a large reference document (for animators and technical animators) the MATHEMATICAL MODELS of human walking and running that can be used to understand, generate or check walk/run animation. Search the web thoroughly (load WebSearch/WebFetch via ToolSearch if they are deferred). Open the pages you take formulas and numbers from (PubMed Central, arXiv, university lecture notes, Wikipedia for orientation only, original papers' open copies). Do NOT write any files; return everything in your final message.

For each model give: the idea in two sentences, the key equations (written plainly or as LaTeX), the parameter values for an adult human, what it predicts that matters to an animator (where the body is highest/lowest, how stride/cadence change with speed, maximum walking speed, etc.), its limits, and the source URL.

1. The inverted pendulum model of walking (compass gait, rimless wheel): energy exchange between potential and kinetic energy, percentage energy recovery (~65% at optimal speed — Cavagna), maximum walking speed v_max = sqrt(g*L) and why people switch to running before it; step-to-step transition cost (Kuo, Donelan & Ruina 2002 "dynamic walking").
2. The spring-loaded inverted pendulum (SLIP) / spring-mass model of running (Blickhan 1989, McMahon & Cheng 1990): equations of motion, leg stiffness k_leg and vertical stiffness k_vert definitions and human values, stance time vs stiffness, angle of attack, the bipedal SLIP that also reproduces walking (Geyer, Seyfarth & Blickhan 2006) and its double-hump force.
3. Froude number Fr = v^2/(g*L) and dynamic similarity (Alexander): walk-run transition at Fr ~0.5, preferred walking at Fr ~0.25; stride length / leg length as a function of Fr (Alexander's relative stride length formula, e.g. lambda/h = 2.3 * Fr^0.3); how to scale a walk to a character of a different size (a giant, a child, a mouse) — speed scales with sqrt(L), cycle time with sqrt(L/g).
4. Empirical relations: step length vs height (e.g. ~0.41-0.45 x height), cadence vs speed, the walk ratio (step length / cadence ~0.0065 m/(steps/min) constant across speeds — Sekiya & Nagasaki), Grieve's relations, speed = step length x cadence, stride length vs speed power laws, preferred step frequency and its pendulum explanation (natural frequency of the leg as a pendulum: T = 2*pi*sqrt(I/(m*g*d))), metabolic cost of transport curve (U-shaped, minimum ~1.3 m/s).
5. Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM, Kajita), the Zero Moment Point (ZMP), capture point / divergent component of motion (Pratt, Englsberger) — the closed-form solution x(t) = x0 cosh(t/Tc) + Tc*v0 sinh(t/Tc), Tc = sqrt(z/g); how COM moves sideways over alternating feet (the lateral sway as a LIPM oscillation); usefulness for procedural balance in animation.
6. Fourier / harmonic description of gait: joint angles as low-order Fourier series (how many harmonics reproduce hip/knee/ankle; Unuma, Anjyo & Takeuchi 1995 SIGGRAPH "Fourier principles for emotion-based human figure animation" — interpolating and exaggerating walks in the frequency domain; Bruderlin & Williams 1995 "Motion signal processing").
7. Procedural walk generators in computer animation: Bruderlin & Calvert 1989 (KLAW, "Goal-directed dynamic animation of human walking") and 1996 "Knowledge-driven, interactive animation of human running"; Boulic, Magnenat-Thalmann & Thalmann 1990 "A global human walking model with real-time kinematic personification" (normalized velocity, relative step length/duration formulas); Multon et al. review 1999 "Computer animation of human walking: a survey"; Sun & Metaxas 2001 automating gait generation; central pattern generators (Taga 1991, Matsuoka oscillator) briefly; Kovar et al. footskate cleanup.
8. Physics-based / learned: SIMBICON (Yin et al. 2007), DeepMimic (Peng 2018) — one paragraph each; Phase-Functioned Neural Networks (Holden 2017), motion matching (Clavet 2016) — a paragraph each.
9. Useful small models for animators: the foot trajectory in swing (minimum-jerk or cycloid-like curves; the swing as a pendulum; foot clearance), the pelvis vertical path as a sinusoid at twice the step frequency (amplitude ~ L*(1-cos(theta)) from leg angle theta), lateral sway amplitude formula from step width, arm swing as a passive pendulum (natural period of the arm vs stride period).

Output: compact markdown organised by the numbered items, LaTeX-friendly equations, a table of human parameter values (with units) per model, and the URL of each page used beside the facts. Mark anything you could not confirm. Up to ~4500 words. Write in English.
